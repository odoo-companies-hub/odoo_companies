Odoo Glossary

The terms that come up most when you're evaluating partners or scoping a project, explained without the marketing language.

Jump to a term, or scroll through all 24.

Apps

Odoo's name for its individual business modules — Sales, Inventory, Accounting, Manufacturing, HR, and dozens more. You install only the apps your business needs, and they share one underlying database so data flows between them automatically (a sale updates stock, which updates accounting). See the modules guide for a full tour.

Database

Your company's own isolated copy of Odoo — all your data, settings, and installed apps live in one database. Most businesses run a single production database plus a separate test/staging database a partner uses to try changes before applying them live.

Studio

An Enterprise-only, no-code tool for customizing Odoo yourself — adding fields, changing forms, building simple automations — without writing Python. It covers a lot of common customization requests, but anything deeper (custom logic, integrations with outside systems) still needs a developer.

Odoo.sh

Odoo's own cloud hosting platform, aimed at businesses with custom code. It gives you proper staging branches, automated testing, and git-based deployment — closer to how a software team would normally ship changes — versus the simpler "Odoo Online" hosting tier, which doesn't support custom code at all.

Community Edition

Odoo's free, open-source edition. It covers the core of most apps and can be self-hosted at no license cost, but is missing some Enterprise-only features (Studio, advanced manufacturing/accounting tools, official support). See the full Community vs Enterprise comparison.

Enterprise Edition

Odoo's paid edition, licensed per user per month. It adds features like Studio, more advanced reporting, and official Odoo support, and is required to use Odoo's own cloud hosting (Odoo Online / Odoo.sh).

Multi-company

A setup where one Odoo database manages several legally separate companies (e.g. a group with subsidiaries in different countries), with data optionally shared or kept strictly separate between them. It's more complex to configure correctly than a single-company setup — worth asking a prospective partner directly about their multi-company experience if this applies to you.

Fiscal localization

The country-specific tax rules, chart of accounts, and reporting formats Odoo ships for each country (VAT handling, e-invoicing formats, statutory reports). A partner based in your country, or with real experience in your country's localization, will usually get this right faster than one guessing from general accounting knowledge.

Chart of accounts

The structured list of accounts (assets, liabilities, income, expenses) your accounting is built on. Odoo installs a default chart based on your country's fiscal localization, which most businesses adjust slightly rather than rebuild from scratch.

Kanban view

The card-based, drag-to-move-between-columns screen style Odoo uses throughout the system — a sales pipeline, a hiring pipeline, a manufacturing queue. It's one of several ways to view the same underlying records (list, calendar, pivot table, and Kanban all show the same data differently).

Pipeline (CRM)

The sequence of stages a sales opportunity moves through — New, Qualified, Proposal, Won/Lost, for example. Odoo's CRM app is built around this Kanban-style pipeline view, and it's usually one of the first things configured in a sales-focused implementation.

POS (Point of Sale)

Odoo's retail checkout app — works on a browser, tablet, or dedicated POS hardware, and stays usable offline with sync once reconnected. It integrates directly with Inventory and Accounting, so a sale at the till updates stock and the books without manual re-entry.

MRP (Manufacturing)

Odoo's manufacturing app, covering production orders, work centers, and scheduling. The name comes from "Material Requirements Planning," a standard manufacturing-software concept; Odoo's version ranges from simple assembly tracking to fairly advanced multi-step routing.

BOM (Bill of Materials)

The recipe for a manufactured product — which components and quantities go into making one unit. Every manufactured item in Odoo needs a BOM defined before it can be produced, and BOM accuracy directly affects whether your stock and costing numbers are trustworthy.

Automated / server actions

Odoo's built-in automation layer — "when X happens, do Y" rules configured through the interface (e.g. auto-email a customer when an order ships) without custom code. Studio and the base automation app both expose this; more complex automations still need a developer.

Website builder

Odoo's drag-and-drop site builder, which can run a full website, blog, and eCommerce store connected directly to Inventory and Sales. It's a reasonable option if you want your site and backend in one system, though it's less flexible than a dedicated website platform for highly custom front-end designs.

Helpdesk

Odoo's customer-support ticketing app — tracks support tickets through stages, SLAs, and team assignment, and links back to the customer's sales and project history since it shares the same database as everything else.

Subscriptions

Odoo's recurring-billing app, for businesses that invoice customers on a repeating schedule (monthly SaaS fees, maintenance contracts). It handles automatic renewal, proration, and upgrade/downgrade billing logic that would otherwise be manual.

Data migration

Moving your existing records — customers, products, open invoices, historical transactions — into Odoo from whatever system you're replacing. This is usually the most underestimated part of a project; ask any partner exactly what data they'll migrate for you versus what you'll need to re-enter manually.

Go-live

The date your business actually starts running on Odoo day-to-day, instead of the old system. Most implementations run a short parallel period or a hard cutover on a specific date — get clear, in writing, on which approach your partner plans to use and what support is available in the first weeks after.

Named user

Odoo Enterprise is licensed per named (individual) user, not per concurrent session — so a 20-person team generally needs 20 licenses even if not everyone is logged in simultaneously. This is a key input into the cost estimates in our implementation cost guide.

Third-party apps

Additional modules built by other developers (available on Odoo's App Store or from the open-source OCA community) that extend Odoo beyond its official apps. Quality varies widely — a good partner will vet a third-party app's reviews, maintenance history, and compatibility with your Odoo version before installing it on your production database.

API (XML-RPC / JSON-RPC)

The programmatic interface Odoo exposes so external systems (an e-commerce platform, a shipping carrier, a custom app) can read and write data in your database. Most integrations a partner builds for you go through this API rather than touching the database directly.

Inventory valuation

The accounting method used to value stock on hand — commonly FIFO (first in, first out) or average cost in Odoo. The right choice depends on your industry and local accounting rules, and changing it after go-live is disruptive, so it's worth getting right during setup rather than fixing later.

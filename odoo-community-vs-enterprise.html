Odoo Community vs Enterprise: Key Differences

Odoo comes in two editions. Here's what actually changes between them, beyond "one is free."

Odoo Community is free, open-source, and self-hostable — you can download it and run it yourself with no license fee. Odoo Enterprise is a paid, per-user/per-app subscription. The difference is more than price: it affects which apps you get, how you host it, and what support looks like.

Side-by-side

CommunityEnterprise
CostFreePaid subscription (see odoo.com/pricing for current rates)
HostingSelf-hosted onlySelf-hosted, Odoo.sh, or Odoo Online (Odoo's own managed cloud)
App coverageCore apps (Sales, CRM, Inventory, basic Accounting, etc.)Everything in Community plus additional apps (advanced Manufacturing features, Odoo's own support desk, some HR/Payroll features, and others that change release to release)
Official supportCommunity forums only — no official Odoo supportOfficial Odoo support included
UpgradesYou (or your partner) manage upgrades manuallyOdoo-managed upgrade path available on Odoo.sh/Online
Source accessFully open-source, freely modifiableEnterprise-specific modules use a proprietary license — modifiable under your subscription, but not freely redistributable

Who typically chooses Community

  • Businesses with in-house developers comfortable managing servers, updates, and backups themselves
  • Very cost-sensitive startups validating whether Odoo fits before investing further
  • Developers building a heavily customized vertical product on top of Odoo's framework

Who typically chooses Enterprise

  • Businesses that want official support and a managed upgrade path instead of handling it themselves
  • Teams that need apps only available in Enterprise (check the current app list, as this changes over time)
  • Companies that would rather pay a predictable subscription than carry the technical risk of self-hosting
Practical tip: Which edition an implementation partner recommends is itself useful signal — a partner who defaults to Enterprise without asking about your technical capacity or budget may be optimizing for their own margin rather than your situation. It's reasonable to ask them to justify the recommendation for your specific case.

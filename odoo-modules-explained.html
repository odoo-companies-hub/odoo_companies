Odoo Modules Explained: A Plain-English Overview

Odoo is organized into "apps" that share one database. Here's what the main ones actually do, in plain terms.

Odoo's biggest structural idea is that every app — Sales, Inventory, Accounting, and so on — writes to the same underlying database. A sales order in the Sales app automatically becomes a delivery in Inventory and an invoice in Accounting, without re-entering data. Here's a plain-English tour of the apps most businesses start with.

Core business apps

Sales & CRM

CRM tracks leads and opportunities through a pipeline; Sales turns a won opportunity into a quote, then a confirmed sales order. These two are usually the first apps a business turns on.

Inventory

Tracks stock across one or multiple warehouses, handles receiving and shipping, and supports advanced routing (e.g. pick-pack-ship, dropshipping, cross-docking) for businesses that need it. Deeply linked to Sales and Manufacturing.

Manufacturing (MRP)

Bills of materials, work orders, and production scheduling. Relevant only to businesses that actually make products rather than just buy and resell them.

Accounting / Invoicing

Handles invoicing, vendor bills, bank reconciliation, and financial reporting. In many countries Odoo ships localized versions (tax rules, e-invoicing formats) — worth confirming your country's localization is mature before committing, which is one reason to pick a partner with local experience (see the partner directory filtered by country).

Purchase

Manages vendor quotations, purchase orders, and incoming goods — the mirror image of Sales, from the buying side.

Operations and people apps

  • HR / Employees — organizational structure, time off, appraisals
  • Payroll — country-specific, so maturity varies a lot by region
  • Project — task and project tracking, often used alongside Timesheets for billable work
  • Helpdesk — support ticket management

Customer-facing apps

  • Website — a built-in CMS/website builder
  • eCommerce — an online store that shares the same product and inventory data as the rest of Odoo, so stock levels and orders stay in sync automatically
  • Point of Sale (POS) — in-person retail checkout, including offline support for unreliable internet connections

How to think about scope

You don't need to implement every app on day one. A very common pattern is: start with Sales + Inventory + Accounting (sometimes called the "quote-to-cash" core), get that running well, then add Manufacturing, HR, or Website in a later phase once the team is comfortable with the basics.

Practical tip: More apps means more configuration and training, which means more cost and more risk of overwhelming your team. Ask a prospective partner to recommend a minimal phase-one scope rather than everything at once — a good partner will push back on over-scoping, not encourage it.

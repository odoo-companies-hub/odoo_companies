How to Choose an Odoo Partner: A Practical Checklist

The partner you pick matters more than the software itself. Here's what to actually check before signing.

Odoo's implementation quality varies enormously by partner — the same software can deliver a smooth, well-adopted system or a frustrating, half-finished one depending entirely on who configures it. Here's a practical checklist.

1. Match tier to project complexity

Odoo partners are ranked Gold, Silver, and Ready based on experience and scale. Gold partners suit larger or more complex rollouts; a well-matched Silver or Ready partner is often more cost-effective for a straightforward, standard-module implementation. Tier isn't a guarantee of quality on its own — it's a starting filter, not the final word.

2. Ask for references in your industry and size

A partner that's great for a 200-person manufacturer isn't necessarily right for a 15-person services firm. Ask for 2-3 references from businesses similar to yours, and actually call them — ask specifically what went wrong, not just what went right.

3. Get a fixed-scope quote for phase one

Open-ended hourly estimates are hard to compare and easy to overrun. Ask for a fixed quote covering a clearly defined phase one (e.g. "Sales + Inventory + Accounting, standard configuration, data migration for products and open invoices only"). This also tests whether the partner can actually scope a project clearly.

4. Clarify what happens after go-live

Ask directly: What support is included after launch? What's the hourly rate for changes afterward? Who handles version upgrades? A partner that goes quiet after go-live is a common complaint — get this in writing before signing.

5. Check country/timezone fit

If you need responsive support during your business hours, a partner on the other side of the world may struggle regardless of how skilled they are. Conversely, many businesses deliberately choose a partner in a lower-cost region and manage the time difference, trading some responsiveness for lower rates. Decide which tradeoff fits you before you start comparing quotes — use the partner directory to filter by country.

Red flags

  • Won't provide any customer references
  • Refuses to quote a fixed scope for phase one, insists on open-ended hourly only
  • Recommends Enterprise edition and extensive customization without first asking about your actual processes and budget
  • Vague about post-launch support terms
Practical tip: It's reasonable — and common — to get quotes from 2-3 partners for the same phase-one scope before deciding. The spread in quotes and the quality of each partner's questions during scoping tells you a lot about how the project will actually go.

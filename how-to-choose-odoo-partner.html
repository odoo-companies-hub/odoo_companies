How to Choose an Odoo Partner: A Practical Checklist

The partner you pick matters more than the software itself. Here's what to actually check before signing.

Odoo's implementation quality varies enormously by partner — the same software can deliver a smooth, well-adopted system or a frustrating, half-finished one depending entirely on who configures it. Here's a practical checklist.

1. Match tier to project complexity

Odoo partners are ranked Gold, Silver, and Ready based on experience and scale. Gold partners suit larger or more complex rollouts; a well-matched Silver or Ready partner is often more cost-effective for a straightforward, standard-module implementation. Tier isn't a guarantee of quality on its own — it's a starting filter, not the final word.

2. Ask for references in your industry and size

A partner that's great for a 200-person manufacturer isn't necessarily right for a 15-person services firm. Ask for 2-3 references from businesses similar to yours, and actually call them — ask specifically what went wrong, not just what went right.

3. Get a fixed-scope quote for phase one

Open-ended hourly estimates are hard to compare and easy to overrun. Ask for a fixed quote covering a clearly defined phase one (e.g. "Sales + Inventory + Accounting, standard configuration, data migration for products and open invoices only"). This also tests whether the partner can actually scope a project clearly.

4. Clarify what happens after go-live

Ask directly: What support is included after launch? What's the hourly rate for changes afterward? Who handles version upgrades? A partner that goes quiet after go-live is a common complaint — get this in writing before signing.

5. Check country/timezone fit

If you need responsive support during your business hours, a partner on the other side of the world may struggle regardless of how skilled they are. Conversely, many businesses deliberately choose a partner in a lower-cost region and manage the time difference, trading some responsiveness for lower rates. Decide which tradeoff fits you before you start comparing quotes — use the partner directory to filter by country.

Gold vs Silver vs Ready: what the tiers actually mean in practice

Odoo's partner tiers are based on criteria like number of certified consultants, completed projects, and revenue through the Odoo ecosystem — they're a reasonable proxy for scale and experience, but not a direct measure of how well a specific partner will handle your specific project.

  • Gold partners typically have larger teams, more specialized consultants (e.g. dedicated Manufacturing or multi-country specialists), and more bandwidth for complex, multi-phase rollouts — usually at higher rates.
  • Silver partners are often a sweet spot for mid-size projects: enough experience to handle real complexity, typically more flexible scheduling and pricing than a large Gold shop.
  • Ready partners are newer to the Odoo ecosystem. Some are excellent and simply haven't accumulated the project volume yet; others are genuinely inexperienced. Weight references and a trial conversation more heavily than tier alone when evaluating a Ready partner.

Questions to ask on the first discovery call

  1. "Walk me through a project similar to mine, start to finish — what went well, and what would you do differently now?" (Tests both relevant experience and honesty.)
  2. "Who specifically will be working on our project day-to-day?" (Sales engineers often aren't the consultants who'll actually do the work — ask to meet the real team.)
  3. "What does your data migration process look like, concretely?" (A vague answer here is a strong signal of risk, since migration is where many projects go over budget.)
  4. "What's your typical timeline from kickoff to go-live for a project like mine, and what usually causes delays?" (Partners who've delivered real projects can answer specifically; ones who can't are often newer than they present.)
  5. "What happens if we need to change scope mid-project?" (Clarifies whether change requests are handled fairly or used to pad the invoice.)

Red flags

  • Won't provide any customer references
  • Refuses to quote a fixed scope for phase one, insists on open-ended hourly only
  • Recommends Enterprise edition and extensive customization without first asking about your actual processes and budget
  • Vague about post-launch support terms
Practical tip: It's reasonable — and common — to get quotes from 2-3 partners for the same phase-one scope before deciding. The spread in quotes and the quality of each partner's questions during scoping tells you a lot about how the project will actually go.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Gold partner always better than a Silver or Ready partner?

No — tier reflects scale and track record in the Odoo ecosystem, not a guarantee of fit for your specific project. A well-matched Silver or Ready partner with relevant industry references is often a better choice for a straightforward, standard-scope implementation than an oversized Gold partner.

How many partners should I get quotes from?

2-3 is usually enough to see meaningful variation in approach and pricing without dragging the process out. Make sure each quote covers the exact same phase-one scope so you're comparing like for like — see Odoo Implementation Cost for how to structure that ask.

Should I choose a local partner or a remote/offshore one?

It depends on how much you value in-timezone responsiveness versus lower rates. Neither is inherently "better" — use the partner directory to compare partners by country and weigh the tradeoff against your own support expectations.

What's the single biggest mistake businesses make when picking a partner?

Choosing on price alone without checking references or getting a fixed, itemized scope. The cheapest quote is often cheap because it excludes data migration, training, or post-launch support — costs that don't disappear, they just show up later as change requests.
